Your Gut is Your Second Brain
Key Takeaway

Your gut contains over 100 million neurons and produces 95 percent of your body's serotonin. When your microbiome is disrupted, it does not just affect digestion — it sends distress signals directly to your brain, triggering anxiety, brain fog, and low mood. Healing your gut is one of the most powerful things you can do for your mental wellbeing.

You wake up feeling a vague, heavy sense of anxiety. There is no logical reason for it — your career is stable, your relationships are healthy, and your schedule is manageable. But you are looking in the completely wrong organ. Emerging science reveals that this emotional turbulence frequently originates much lower down — inside your digestive tract.

Meet Your Enteric Nervous System

🔬
Why Scientists Call It the Second Brain

For decades, we treated the gut as a simple processing tube meant only to digest food and eliminate waste. Today, neuroscientists refer to the gut as the Second Brain. Your gut is lined with over 100 million neurons — more than your entire spinal cord — forming a complex network known as the enteric nervous system.

This system communicates directly with your brain via the vagus nerve, creating a bidirectional superhighway called the gut-brain axis. Fascinatingly, this highway is not a one-way street. Roughly 90 percent of the nerve fibres in the vagus nerve are sending information upward from your gut to your brain — not the other way around. Your gut is, in very real terms, constantly talking to your mind.

The enteric nervous system can function completely independently of the brain — it has its own reflexes and can sense, process, and respond to information without any input from the central nervous system. This is why gut feelings are neurologically real.
🦠
Your Microbiome — The Chemical Factories Within

The true masters of your daily mood are the trillions of microbes living inside you, known as your gut microbiome. These bacteria are extraordinarily sophisticated chemical factories. They produce more than 30 different neurotransmitters, including an astonishing 95 percent of your body's total supply of serotonin — the chemical most responsible for feelings of happiness, stability, and emotional calm.

When your microbiome becomes unbalanced — a state called dysbiosis — caused by a poor diet, processed foods, chronic stress, or overuse of antibiotics, the production of these mood-boosting chemicals plummets. Instead of sending signals of safety and contentment upward through the vagus nerve, an inflamed gut sends distress signals to your brain, directly triggering anxiety, brain fog, and depressive symptoms.

The Three-Colour Rule — Feeding Your Second Brain

Healing your mood requires healing your ecosystem. To rebalance your second brain, you do not need an aggressive medical cleanse or an expensive supplement stack. Instead, begin with one powerful nutritional habit — the Three-Colour Rule.

Every single day, challenge yourself to eat three naturally distinct colours of plant-based foods. These varied colours represent diverse polyphenols and fibres, which act as specific prebiotics to feed different strains of beneficial gut bacteria. By intentionally diversifying your plate, you soothe the vagus nerve, reduce gut inflammation, and begin restoring your brain's serotonin supply from the inside out.

Frequently Asked Questions

How quickly can improving gut health affect mood?
Dietary changes can begin shifting gut microbiome composition within three to four days. However, meaningful improvements in mood-related symptoms typically take four to eight weeks of consistent dietary changes. The key is sustained improvement rather than rapid intervention — the microbiome responds to patterns, not individual meals.
Can gut health affect anxiety specifically?
Yes — significantly. Research shows that the gut microbiome influences GABA pathways — the brain's primary calming neurotransmitter system. Disrupted microbiomes are associated with reduced GABA signalling, which is directly linked to heightened anxiety responses. Multiple clinical studies have found that probiotic supplementation and dietary improvements reduced self-reported anxiety scores in healthy adults.
Is the Three-Colour Rule evidence-based?
The Three-Colour Rule is a practical application of well-established nutritional science. Research consistently shows that dietary diversity — measured by the variety of different plant foods consumed — is one of the strongest predictors of gut microbiome diversity. The American Gut Project, one of the largest citizen science studies, found that eating 30 or more different plant foods per week was associated with significantly greater microbiome diversity than eating 10 or fewer.
Should I take a probiotic supplement?
Probiotic supplements can be a useful addition, particularly after antibiotic use or during periods of digestive disruption. However, evidence suggests that dietary diversity — consistently eating a wide variety of whole plant foods — produces broader and more sustainable microbiome improvements than single-strain probiotic supplements. Food-based approaches are generally preferable as a foundation, with supplements used to address specific gaps.
